03 · Publish

Put it online with Netlify Drop.

  1. Ask for a zip. The website prompt tells your AI to return one downloadable zip with index.html, style.css, syncnet.json and the images. Open index.html on your computer first and read every sentence.
  2. Drop it. Go to app.netlify.com/drop ↗ and drag the zip onto the page. You get a live URL in seconds.
  3. Claim it. Sign up or log in (free) to keep the site. Until then Netlify protects it with a temporary password.
  4. Copy the URL — before you launch. The website field in your token metadata is permanent. Publish first, then paste the final URL into the SyncNet builder, or leave the field empty.
  5. Update later by dropping again. After launch, add the token contract here, regenerate syncnet.json, and drop the updated folder on the same Netlify project (Deploys → drag and drop).
What you should know

The site is yours: your Netlify account, your domain, your responsibility. Netlify’s free plan pauses sites that exceed its monthly credits. SyncNet does not host, review or endorse the site; it only reads the declaration file below.

04 · Link it to SyncNet

Declare the site. Let SyncNet check it.

Put this file at the root of your site as syncnet.json. Your project page on SyncNet can then check both directions: the token metadata points to the site, and the site points back to the token. Only when both match does SyncNet show WEBSITE LINKED ✓. A site that only declares itself is shown as unconfirmed.

Why no built-in AI?

A hosted AI would cost money per request, need secret keys and could be abused by anyone. Your own AI is free for you, stronger than a small in-browser model and works on every device. SyncNet supplies the facts and the rules; the writing stays yours.

Your words, your responsibility

AI output can be wrong. Never publish a contract address, market or claim you have not checked yourself. A market connection is not a partnership, endorsement or promise of returns.
